How it works

A short tour of what happens between pasting a link and downloading your notes.

  1. Fetch the transcript

    The subtitles of the video are downloaded (Urdu, Hindi or English, manual or automatic) and grouped into one-minute blocks, each keeping its timestamp.

  2. Split into chunks

    The minutes are grouped into chunks of about five minutes. Smaller chunks make it far less likely that the AI skips anything.

  3. Convert with Gemini

    Each chunk is sent to Google Gemini with strict instructions: write every sentence in the language you chose (clear English, or Roman Urdu), do not summarise, do not merge, do not skip, and organise the result into headings, lists, tables and callout boxes.

  4. Check and retry

    The number of words in the output is compared with the source. A chunk that falls below the coverage threshold is retried, and if needed split into smaller parts and converted again. If one model is rate limited, the next available model is tried.

  5. Build the document

    The text is cleaned up and rendered into a styled Word document with a header and footer, coloured callouts, tables, bolded key terms, clickable timestamps and an outline at the end. A plain text copy is saved too.

What you need

  • A free account. Continue with Google, or register with your Gmail address and a password.
  • A YouTube video with captions. Automatic captions are fine. Live streams usually get captions only after they end.
  • A free Google Gemini API key. Open aistudio.google.com/apikey, sign in, choose Create API key and copy the key.

What affects the speed

  • Video length. A longer video means more chunks to convert.
  • Gemini limits. Free keys have rate limits. When they are reached the tool waits and retries automatically.
  • Queue. To keep things fair, only a limited number of jobs run at once. If the server is busy your job waits and shows its position.
